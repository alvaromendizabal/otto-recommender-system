## Scientific question and reason to investigate

Does a candidate's progression through different actions in similar historical sessions supply useful evidence beyond separate action counts and current-prefix behavior? Observing a product clicked and subsequently carted is different from observing it clicked in one session and carted in another. An order followed by a click is also not the same retained sequence as a click followed by an order. Separate marginal counts can hide both joint occurrence and chronology. This representation exposes these distinctions to the unchanged ranker.

The proposed evidence is historical, same-product, and conditioned on a query-specific neighborhood. It is not an assumption that every user follows a linear shopping funnel. There may be direct orders, repeated clicks after orders, multi-device events, missing upstream events, or truncation. No cancellation, removal, inventory, price, brand, product taxonomy, persistent customer identity, or verified abandonment field is inferred. A zero means that the selected retained historical evidence did not support a pattern; it does not mean a shopper disliked a product or certainly failed to convert.

Multi-behavior session recommendation research motivates preserving behavior type and context. In particular, Shen et al. discuss multi-behavior transition structure and the contextual noise of global item graphs. Qin et al. discuss differences between behavior types in session representation. These motivate a class of information, not this package's numerical recipe. We are not reproducing either paper's neural network, using their benchmark scores as an OTTO estimate, or claiming that a hand-designed feature family is already among the most predictive features on this project. Its usefulness must be measured here.

## Distinction from earlier rounds

Round 05 used global directed adjacent transitions between different products with source and destination action labels. Round 08 aggregated candidate support over similar sessions and tested rarity weighting. The pending Round 09 uses the latest query product as a pivot and measures a different product's continuation in a short original-position window. Round 10 instead tests two action types occurring on the SAME candidate product within a historical session. No latest-anchor pivot or maximum event-position gap is imposed on these same-product action pairs. Intervening shopping activity is allowed, subject to a fixed thirty-minute elapsed-time window.

Current-prefix funnel features remain in the saved control; they are not newly invented here. Round 10 derives additional joint-action statistics from the already certified historical neighborhood. The existing query's own future events, supervised targets and study sessions are never used to construct that neighborhood or the new features. Failed demand, timing, affinity-rank and rarity features are not reintroduced as retained predictors. An internal code library may contain their earlier functions for reproducibility, but this experiment's feature-name registry contains only the declared 24-column progression family and its 24-column ablation.

## Frozen neighbors and exact source

Use the completed Round 08 shared cache. It records 321,459 retained historical sessions and 5,797,252 retained events, with 5,120 study-session exclusions. Those are receipt-reported counts; source integrity is checked again against the actual private arrays. The same cache selected at most the 64 most recent eligible historical sessions per query anchor. These are not exhaustive globally nearest neighbors, and their retained event tails are not complete original shopping sessions. This sampling can omit useful context and imposes a real limitation on every result.

A query uses its most recent four distinct observed products, or all available products when fewer exist. Their weights are reciprocal recency ranks, without Round 08's rejected rarity adjustment. A historical session has a binary vector over its unique retained products. Similarity is the dot product with the weighted query vector divided by their two Euclidean norms. The union of the available anchor postings forms the candidate historical-session pool. Sort by descending similarity, then descending retained end timestamp, then ascending session ID, and retain at most 64. The primary and ablation use exactly the same neighbors, similarity values and neighbor ranks.

Historical rows must have timestamps strictly less than 1660687200000 milliseconds, or 2022-08-16 22:00 UTC. Both endpoints of every progression must lie in this certified history. Original event indices determine chronology when timestamps tie. No row filtering is allowed to compress original positions into false adjacency. Round 10 does not scan raw JSON, rescan the historical Parquet source, regenerate postings, refit the original graphs, or build a new persistent index. It may read and hash existing files to verify them; absence of a new extraction scan does not imply zero I/O cost.

## Primary and width-matched ablation

The three source/destination pairs are click to cart, cart to order, and click to order. For one candidate product and one selected historical session, the primary qualifies when some retained source-action event precedes a retained destination-action event in ORIGINAL index order and their timestamp difference is between zero and thirty minutes, inclusive. Use the smallest eligible elapsed gap. The ablation requires the same two action types on the same product and the same absolute elapsed-time limit, but removes the index-direction requirement. A reverse sequence can therefore support only the ablation. Equal timestamps may support the primary only when original indices have the correct strict order.

Repeated events never add multiple support votes for the same historical session, product, and action pair. For example, five orders following a cart still represent one supporting historical session. Repeated events can legitimately change the minimum eligible elapsed gap, but they cannot mechanically multiply counts or similarity mass. The minimum-gap implementation uses ordered scans, not an unbounded quadratic cross join. A separate exhaustive tiny-fixture oracle checks this implementation against random sequences with repeated and tied timestamps.

For each of the three action pairs, both arms create these eight measurements: log distinct supporting-session count; total similarity-weighted pair share; pair-support count divided by source-action support plus twenty; similarity-weighted support conditional on source-action support; strongest supporting similarity; reciprocal-neighbor-rank support share; similarity-weighted exponentially lag-decayed support share; and log effective supporting-session count. This is exactly twenty-four columns per arm. No combined forty-eight-column challenger is fitted. Every formula and zero convention is in feature_catalog.json.

The global similarity and reciprocal-rank denominators include all selected neighbors. The source-action denominator is candidate-specific and includes selected sessions with the relevant source action even when no qualifying destination action appears. It does not require that the product be a query anchor, does not use future outcomes, and does not omit neighbors just because their candidate failed to progress. The count-shrinkage constant is twenty. The time-decay scale is five minutes and the maximum elapsed window is thirty minutes. These constants are preregistered and are not swept against the repeatedly inspected fitting cohort.

The conditional ratios are empirical support intensities in a selected, retained-history sample. They are not causal conversion effects, calibrated purchase probabilities, exposure-corrected estimates or unbiased population rates. Effective support is the square of the sum of supporting similarities divided by the sum of their squares, then transformed with log one plus. It summarizes concentration of observed support and is not an effective sample-size theorem accounting for correlated shoppers. These qualifications matter when interpreting feature importance or attractive-looking charts.

## Fixed supervised comparison and leakage controls

The original 4,096 fitting sessions, full 400-candidate pools and all 134 control columns remain unchanged and in their original order. The primary and ablation each produce 158-column challengers. Features are committed before supervised labels are accessed. Construct them for all candidates before label-dependent negative sampling. Targets in chronological training data are censored before the validation cutoff; the original six-hour query embargo, sixty-negative budget, sampling seed, model seed, LambdaRank parameters and 150 boosting rounds are preserved. Validation always uses complete candidate pools and original capped target denominators.

Replay all six saved controls against the exact validation matrices before fitting the first challenger. A changed model file, feature order, input digest, sampled row identity, target ledger, fold membership or per-session control hit causes a stop. Each new native model is saved with an immutable input contract and must reproduce its predictions after reload. At most twelve new experiment models are fitted: two representations, two chronological folds and three target objectives. The six controls are never refitted. Synthetic unit tests may fit tiny fixture models; these are explicitly distinct from the twelve real-data experiment models.

This is another exploratory comparison on a repeatedly reused fitting cohort, not untouched validation or independent confirmation. Selection-role data, evaluation-role data and competition tests are not accessed. Training-positive support and correlations are descriptive diagnostics based only on appropriately censored training labels, not a feature-selection loop. All twenty-four declared columns are kept for the planned comparison even when a diagnostic suggests redundancy. No column, smoothing constant, window or model parameter is chosen after seeing this round's validation outcomes.

## Evaluation, interpretation and advancement

The internal metric follows the weighted recall form: clicks weight 0.1, carts 0.3 and orders 0.6, with each query/objective target denominator capped at twenty. Compute pooled ratios from integer hits and complete denominators rather than averaging fold scores. Report pooled and fold-specific scores, objective recalls, exact extra hits, candidate-oracle ceilings, training-positive support and correlation with the 134 control features. Nine saved Plotly charts make these quantities visible in the results notebook. An oracle measures perfect use of the existing candidate pool, not achieved model performance.

The primary must gain at least 0.003 over the saved control, avoid a negative difference on either chronological fold, and avoid pooled order-recall decline before it earns a proposal for separate confirmation. A win against the ablation alone does not compensate for losing against the control. Two thousand paired resamples within folds provide descriptive intervals; these do not correct for all adaptive choices, multiple tests or temporal dependence. A positive point estimate, even with a favorable interval, is not automatic feature promotion. Freeze any promising representation and test it on a different cohort and suitable different historical time window before retention or submission decisions.

The historical private benchmark target of 0.60503 remains a project goal. A single internal fold exceeding that number does not beat a private leaderboard, and no result in this package is presented that way. Model capacity, training scale, retrieval coverage and validation design have not been proved optimal merely because they are held fixed here. Feature engineering remains the immediate priority, but the evidence must distinguish representation gaps from those other possible limitations rather than attributing every loss to insufficient feature count.

## Execution, preservation and return evidence

Complete the pending Round 09 first; a completed negative result still permits Round 10. An operational failure, timeout or checkpoint pause requires stopping and returning the ZIP, not proceeding or repeatedly retrying. Round 10's launcher shares the existing process lock so the two rounds cannot run concurrently. Its CLI exposes tests, features, screen, report and bundle only. There is no all command and no historical-rebuild stage. A new bounded in-memory summary cache stores at most 32,768 session summaries during feature generation; it is tied to the exact history mapping and discarded after the process ends.

Feature work and screening each have a 240-second useful-work cap and 260-second outer process cap. Tests and reporting have ninety-second outer limits. These are stop limits, not expected runtimes or dollar guarantees. Fifteen-second UTC heartbeats, 64-query feature checkpoints, model counters, file checksums and run receipts show what completed. Every real-data preflight checks at least ten GiB free disk and the launcher stops a worker above twenty-six GiB resident memory. A successful checkpoint may be reused only when its full contract matches; conflicting or orphan evidence is preserved and execution stops.

The installer preserves existing Round 09 sources, notebook outputs, checkpoints, earlier rounds and the repository. It creates only a new Round 10 directory when absent. It installs files rather than dependencies and makes no network, AWS, S3, IAM, Git write or submission calls. The workspace environment is not recreated. Save notebooks before the final bundle commands; return files contain small logs, receipts, statistics and notebook outputs, not the large historical, feature or native-model arrays. Download both return ZIPs before stopping the JupyterLab application. Stopping a kernel or closing the browser is insufficient to stop instance billing; deleting the persistent space would destroy stored files.
